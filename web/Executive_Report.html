FIFA World Cup 2026 Predictor

Tournament forecast report · Academic analytics project · Generated 2026-06-14

Summary. A walk-forward Poisson/Dixon-Coles model estimates international A-team goal rates from a five-year rolling window of match history (recent matches upweighted; club football excluded). Match-level probabilities are validated on held-out 2018 and 2022 World Cup finals (Brier score). Because the 2026 format links group standings to a 32-team knockout bracket — including best-third-place slot assignment — tournament-stage outcomes are estimated with Monte Carlo simulation (10,000 runs). This report presents iteration 8 forecasts against the FIFA official 48-team schedule; external win-probability benchmarks appear later for comparison only.

Methodology

  • Training data: Kaggle martj42 international A-team results; five-year window before each cutoff; 2-year half-life decay.
  • Fixtures: FIFA.com official 2026 schedule (local CSV), with playoff slots resolved from UEFA/FIFA results.
  • Model: Log-linear Poisson goals with tier weights, Elo anchor, Dixon-Coles τ, opponent difficulty, and competition-context weights (iterations 3–7); optional squad-value prior blend (iteration 8 prototype).
  • Validation: Walk-forward — tune on 2018 World Cup, confirm on 2022; 64 finals matches held out per year.
  • DataCamp: competition submission format only (match_id, schema) — not used as fixture authority.

Why Monte Carlo simulation?

Dixon-Coles Poisson yields match-level score probabilities, not tournament-stage outcomes directly. The 2026 format adds twelve groups, eight best-third-place qualifiers mapped to fixed knockout slots, and bracket dependencies that have no closed-form winner probability. We simulate many independent tournaments (10,000), sample scores from the model, apply FIFA tiebreak rules, and aggregate P(win), P(reach each stage), and group-advancement rates.

Brier score & Dixon-Coles

Brier score. For each World Cup match we predict three probabilities (home win, draw, away win). The Brier score is the mean squared error between those probabilities and the actual result (expressed as a one-hot vector). Lower is better: 0 is perfect; about 0.67 is the benchmark from naively guessing one-third for every outcome. We average over all 64 held-out tournament matches to compare iterations and years. Iteration choice for development uses the lowest 2018 Brier; 2022 is reported as confirmation without re-tuning.

Dixon-Coles. Classical Poisson models assume home and away goals are independent; real football shows extra correlation on low scores (0–0, 1–0, 0–1, 1–1), especially draws. Dixon and Coles (1997) multiply independent Poisson probabilities by a correction factor τ (rho, ρ) on those scorelines. Our pipeline learns team attack and defense rates via Poisson negative log-likelihood (iterations 1–4); iteration 5 adds the τ term in training and when converting goal rates to win/draw/loss probabilities. World Cup match scoring uses neutral venues (no home advantage). Elo anchoring (iter 4+) shrinks team strengths toward a stable rating so single shock results do not dominate.

Key findings & limitations

  • Elo anchoring (iter 4+) limits overreaction to isolated shock results in training.
  • Dixon-Coles τ (iter 5+) improves low-score and draw calibration on held-out World Cup matches.
  • Iteration 8 adds Transfermarkt squad-value priors; modest Brier improvement on 2018/2022 — retained for sensitivity analysis.
  • No xG or injury data; neutral venues at World Cup inference; live updates require re-running with a new cutoff.
