FIFA World Cup 2026 Predictor

Academic methodology brief — training data & methods (for methodological review)
Author: Chandra Sekhar · Model: Iteration 7 · Generated June 14, 2026

Summary. We train on international A-team matches only (country vs country), using a 5-year rolling window before each World Cup cutoff. Recent matches are upweighted (2-year half-life decay), not excluded. The model does not use club football (Premier League, Champions League, etc.).

Data sources

  • Historical training: Kaggle martj42 international results (results.csv) — A-team match outcomes from 1872 onward.
  • 2026 fixtures: FIFA.com official schedule/PDF (2026_FIFA_World_Cup_Schedule.csv.csv locally), with playoff slots resolved from UEFA/FIFA playoff results.
  • DataCamp files: used only for competition submission format (match_id alignment, output schema, placeholder team labels for grading) — not authoritative for kickoff times, qualification, or real-world truth.
  • Secondary checks (optional): OpenFootball or TheStatsAPI as machine-readable cross-checks against the FIFA schedule.

What matches are included?

Training rows come from the Kaggle martj42 dataset above, extended through 2026. Teams are nations (France, England, Brazil) — never clubs.

  • Included: World Cup & continental qualifiers, continental finals (Euro, Copa, AFCON, Asian Cup), Nations League, Gold Cup, friendlies, and regional A-team cups — all confederations (UEFA, CONMEBOL, CAF, AFC, CONCACAF, OFC).
  • Excluded: Domestic leagues, UEFA Champions League/Europa League, any club match; matches on/after the forecast cutoff; matches older than 5 years.
  • Validation: Walk-forward — tune on 2018 World Cup (cutoff opening day), confirm on 2022; 64 finals matches held out per year for Brier scoring.

Final sample weight = time × tournament tier × Dopp × Cconf. Model: Dixon-Coles Poisson with Elo anchor; neutral venues at World Cup inference.

Why Monte Carlo simulation?

Dixon-Coles Poisson yields match-level score probabilities — not tournament-stage outcomes directly. The 2026 format adds structural complexity: 12 groups of four, eight best-third-place qualifiers assigned to predetermined knockout slots, and a 32-team bracket where later opponents depend on earlier group results.

  • Bracket dependencies: Knockout pairings follow group standings and best-third tiebreak rules; a closed-form tournament-winner probability is not available.
  • Quantities estimated: P(win), P(reach semifinals), group advancement rates, and uncertainty in best-third qualification.
  • Method: Many independent simulated tournaments (10,000 in production) sample match scores from the Poisson model, apply FIFA tiebreak rules, and aggregate stage outcomes.
